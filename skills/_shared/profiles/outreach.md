# Profile: outreach

**Audience:** educated non-specialists: grant lay summaries, conference abstracts, press,
vulgarisation. **Job:** make a real result understandable without deforming it.

## Voice (wiki-rewrite)

Plain words for jargon, but the jargon term introduced once in parentheses so the reader can look
it up. Concrete before abstract. Analogies allowed, each one's limits stated. Enthusiasm in the
finding, never in the adjectives.

## Variant axes (wiki-rewrite)

1. **Concrete-first**: open on the tangible example or consequence, then generalise.
2. **Analogy-led**: one carrying metaphor, with its breaking point named.
3. **Stakes**: why the reader should care, framed around the open problem.
4. **Minimal-jargon**: zero technical terms; test whether the claim survives the translation.

## Claims (wiki-check)

Checkable: the simplified restatement measured against the underlying result, not against other
popularisations.

Scrutinise hardest:

- **Overclaim through simplification**: a dropped hedge or scope condition is *False* here, not
  *Partially true*. Losing the qualifier is the whole failure mode of this genre.
- **Implied application**: "could lead to a cure/product" where the work supports no such thing.
- **Analogies that smuggle a claim**: the metaphor asserting something the result does not.
- **Quantities made vague**: "dramatically" replacing a modest measured effect.

## Constraints (both)

- Simplify the wording, never the claim.
- No implied benefit the research does not support.
- Match the source document's language.

## Syntax notes (both)

Often lives in the same LaTeX/Typst source as the academic text; the markup still applies even
though the register drops.
