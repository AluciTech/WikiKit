# Profile: academic

**Audience:** reviewers and peers in the field. **Job:** defend a claim.

## Voice (wiki-rewrite)

Formal, precise, hedged exactly as far as the evidence hedges. Every claim
attributed. No promotional adjectives, no rhetorical questions, no second
person. Passive only where the agent is genuinely irrelevant.

## Variant axes (wiki-rewrite)

1. **Concise**: clarity and directness. Cut subordinate clauses, one claim per
   sentence.
2. **Nuanced**: expand theoretical implications, scope conditions or
   methodological caveats.
3. **Transitional**: foreground the bridge between the preceding and following
   paragraphs.
4. **Evidential**: lead with the grounding source, restructure the claim around
   it.

## Claims (wiki-check)

Checkable: every attributed statement, statistic, equation, method description
and citation.

Scrutinise hardest:

- **Hedge drift**: source says "suggests", draft says "demonstrates". This is
  the most common and most damaging failure in this genre. Treat it as
  *Partially true*, never as accurate.
- **Citation misattribution**: the key resolves, but to a paper that does not
  make the claim.
- **Silent rounding** or unit changes in reported figures.
- **Novelty claims** ("first to", "no prior work"): verifiable only against the
  wiki's coverage; mark *Unverifiable* rather than accurate unless the wiki
  explicitly supports it.

## Constraints (both)

- Match the draft's language (e.g., French, English, etc.); detect from context,
  never switch.
- Keep terminology consistent with the wiki's definitions; flag if draft and
  wiki disagree.
- Do not change a claim's strength unless explicitly asked to.

## Syntax notes (both)

Citation and cross-reference density is high here. Reuse existing keys only;
never invent one.
